### WP45-THREAD-SCALING-V1 — chains x threads scaling on a hand-written, an autodiff and a BridgeStan target: driver bit-identical and free of internal contention at reachable concurrency; the shared budget-counter fix is not merged
- Ordered time: 2026-10-24 on `wt/thread-scaling`. Preregistration, protocol, harness and fix F1 were committed at `6a569881`, with the harness binaries following at `e4c5bf56`, both before the first evidence cell. The baseline pilot grid ran 18:31–19:31 local, the profile 19:31–19:35 and the interleaved A/B 19:35–19:50. **All walls are contended pilots**: other agents kept 11–13.5 of 16 cores busy throughout.
- Protocol/config: `STUDIES/thread_scaling_v1/{PREREGISTRATION.md,protocol.json}`. The `Sampler` ran at its 0.2.0 defaults (`Limits::default` = `admit_worst_case`) with Stan-style uniform starts excluded from the wall. Targets: `gauss100` (100-D hand gradient, 1,000/20,000), `ll1000` (owalnuts-autodiff T=1000 local level, 200/200) and `ark` (posteriordb 28f8d3d `arK-arK`, BridgeStan 2.9.0 without STAN_THREADS, Windows owned worker, 500/500). Chains and threads in {1,2,4,8,16}: the full 25-cell grid on `gauss100` and T <= C on the others, three repetitions, rep-major. Per cell: process and system CPU, page faults, per-chain gradients, FNV fingerprints and min bulk ESS. The profile covered allocation counts, a per-thread probe timeline (inside/outside the target, CPU/span, P/E/LP-E placement), an allocator ablation and a shared-CAS bench. A/B: baseline vs F1 binaries interleaved on `gauss100` C=T in {1,4,8,16} and `ll1000` {1,16}, three reps each.
- Seeds: 945101–945103 (calibration 945199), verified unused as standalone numbers.
- Status: all 165 grid cells, all six profile files and all 36 A/B cells were present and ok. The profile driver was stopped once by an operator mistake during step 2; the child finished, the resumed driver refused to overwrite steps 1–2 and ran 3–6. No cell was rerun.
- Outcome: **draws, work and metadata were bit-identical across every thread count, repetition and binary.** Strong scaling at C=16, contended: `gauss100` 2.17/3.16/4.17/4.59x at T=2/4/8/16; `ll1000` 2.14/4.15/4.36/3.88x; `ark` 1.61/2.74/2.73/2.83x. Best ESS/s was 169k (`gauss100` C=T=16), 123 (`ll1000` C=16 T=8) and 145 (`ark` C=T=4). Causes, in order: (1) machine contention — process CPU/wall at T=16 was 2.77/1.88/0.84 and per-thread CPU/span 0.11–0.93; (2) chain-length imbalance — wall tracks the longest chain, max/mean gradients at C=16 were 1.51 (`gauss100`) and 3.05 (`ll1000`), which caps C=T=16 at 10.6x and 5.2x even when idle; (3) E-cores at ~1.2x the P-core CPU per gradient, with the LP-E cores never used; (4) BridgeStan on Windows is serialised and also latency-bound — a rendezvous hand-off per gradient gives CPU/wall 0.25–0.36 at T=1, so more caller threads still help up to T=4. Thread spawn, collection and RNG were < 1 % of wall. The allocator ablation was −4.9 % at C=T=16, within noise, with 2.3–2.8 allocations per gradient on `gauss100`. One real shared written line exists on the default path: `TargetEvaluationBudget::reserve`, a CAS per gradient across all chains. It saturates at ~18 M CAS/s (13x at 16 threads with no work, 1.9x with ~1 µs of work).
- Fix F1 (`BudgetMetering::UnobservedWhenCovered`: `Sampler::run` diagonal/identity path skips the counter when the private budget covers the exact worst case; public metering unchanged; unit test plus `cargo test` and kernel fingerprints pass): wall ratios baseline/fixed were 0.92/1.60/0.65/1.98 at C=T=1/4/8/16 and CPU-per-gradient ratios 1.00/1.00/1.10/0.86. Bit-identical: yes. The >= 1.15 clause was met at C=T=16, where the fixed arm got 4.3 vs 1.7 cores. The no-regression clause failed at C=T=1 (0.92 < 0.952) on contention outliers. **Not merged.**
- Predictions: held — P5 bit identity, P7 hybrid cores (low end), P10 mostly, P1 at T4–T16, P2 at k=8, P6 on `gauss100`, and the P9 outcome. Failed — P4 (`ark` was predicted flat; observed 2.8x), P3 at T2/T4 (super-linear against a starved T=1 baseline), P2 at k=16 (0.36), P6 on `ll1000` (0.056–0.077 vs < 0.05), the `ll1000` imbalance size (3.05 vs 1.2–1.6), and P8 at 64/256 steps.

- Conclusion: supported — the multi-chain driver is correct (thread-count invariant) and has no internal contention visible at the 2–4 effective cores a shared machine gives. Its wall time is bounded by the machine, by the slowest chain and, on BridgeStan on Windows, by hand-off latency. Not supported — merging F1 on this evidence. Not tested — idle-machine scaling at 8–16 truly concurrent threads, where the budget CAS is predicted to matter for sub-µs gradients.
- Next decision: (1) run `RUN-QUIET.md` on an idle box and, if it shows the CAS cost on `gauss100`, preregister F1 again there; (2) document that threads above chains do nothing and that wall follows the slowest chain; (3) consider a lower-latency BridgeStan owner hand-off as a separately qualified change; (4) the ~1.7 µs kernel/driver cost per `gauss100` gradient, with ~2.5 allocations, is the larger single-thread lever for cheap targets.
