Crypto volatility, the full posterior

Stochastic-volatility posteriors over the complete daily history of the five largest cryptocurrencies — every latent volatility state sampled jointly with the model's global parameters, no marginalization, no shortcuts. Sampled with oWALNUTS, a Rust implementation of the within-orbit adaptive leapfrog No-U-Turn sampler, compared head-to-head against NumPyro NUTS and nutpie on the same models and seeds.

Why another sampler

NUTS quietly misses the tail. oWALNUTS doesn't.

Neal's funnel is the canonical hard geometry: a scale parameter ω whose exact marginal is N(0, 3²), so exactly 4.78% of posterior mass sits below ω = −5. NumPyro's NUTS, at either target acceptance, places almost none of it there — while emitting hundreds to thousands of divergences. oWALNUTS, whose leapfrog refines its step within each orbit, recovers the tail exactly, with zero divergences, in a tenth of the wall time. Same target, same seeds, measured — not inferred.

Four backends · shared seeds · shared gates

The comparison, including the losses

Every cell ran 4 chains × 1,000 warmup + 3,000 retained draws against preregistered gates (rank R-hat ≤ 1.01 and bulk ESS ≥ 400 on the primary functionals; zero divergences; depth caps ≤ 1%). Zero divergences occurred in all 40 cells and every healthy pair of backends agrees on the posterior (worst z = 2.29). Wall times are from one shared machine.

Fairness notes: oWALNUTS rows use three fresh seeds each (98001–98003) with an all-seeds-must-pass reading; nutpie/NumPyro reference rows are single-seed (97001), frozen from the v1 study. Rows marked “8 chains pooled” are a preregistered, labeled compute extension: two independent at-budget runs (partner seeds 98011–98013) pooled into one 8-chain estimate — the standard rows come first. Rows marked “fallback B” rerun cells whose chains stuck under the frozen metric with the rebuild-enabled fallback configuration, as preregistered. The PyMC-bridge rows run single-threaded in this study (the compiled PyTensor gradient is not thread-safe on the callable transport), so their wall times are not comparable to the 4-thread native rows; ESS per gradient is the like-for-like figure. Wall times were measured on a shared machine. Gate definitions (identical for every backend, frozen in the v1 preregistration): primary = rank-normalized R-hat ≤ 1.01 and bulk/tail ESS ≥ 400 on μ, h_T, mean(h), plus zero divergences and depth caps ≤ 1% for oWALNUTS cells; globals = R-hat ≤ 1.05 and bulk ESS ≥ 100 on the (φ, σ) ridge pair, preregistered as a weaker gate before any evidence because that ridge bottlenecks every tested sampler. Under a strict 1.01/400 reading of the globals pair, exactly one cell in the whole study passes (an oWALNUTS 8-chain pooled XRP run) and no nutpie/NumPyro reference cell does; the per-cell numbers are all published, including that strict view (“globals_strict_1p01_400” in summary.json).

Honest edges — what did not pass At this draw budget, oWALNUTS misses the primary ESS gate on BTC and ETH (T ≈ 3,150), where NumPyro passes: the two observation-scale globals sit on a correlation ≈ −0.9 ridge, and windowed metric adaptation (nutpie, NumPyro) extracts ~1.5–2× more effective samples from it than our one-shot metric. Three PyMC-bridge seeds stalled from bad starts (R-hat 1.4–1.7) — a start-sensitivity of the frozen metric, reported rather than resampled. The named fix, windowed adaptation, is the next preregistered study.
Run it yourself

Reproduce

Everything on this page is computed from committed, checksummed artifacts. The notebook reproduces the BTC posterior in a few seconds on a laptop:

pip install maturin numba pymc arviz
git clone https://github.com/dhruv575/oWALNUTS && cd oWALNUTS/integrations/python
maturin develop --release
import owalnuts
result = owalnuts.sample(owalnuts.from_pymc(sv_model, gil_free=True), ...)
idata = result.to_inferencedata()   # 94k gradients · 3.5 s · zero divergences
